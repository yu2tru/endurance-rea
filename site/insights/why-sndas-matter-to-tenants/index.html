Why SNDAs Matter to Tenants

A Subordination, Non-Disturbance, and Attornment Agreement keeps your lease rights intact if your landlord’s lender forecloses. Here is how it works and how to secure one.

An SNDA (Subordination, Non-Disturbance, and Attornment Agreement) protects a tenant’s right to stay in its space under its existing lease if the landlord’s lender forecloses. In times of economic uncertainty, it is one of the most valuable documents a commercial tenant can secure.

When landlords face mortgage defaults, lenders may initiate foreclosures, putting tenants at risk of eviction even if they have a valid lease. SNDAs play a pivotal role as a safeguard, ensuring that tenants retain their lease rights even after a foreclosure.

Understanding SNDAs: a shield for tenants

An SNDA is a legally binding contract between three parties: a tenant, a landlord, and the landlord’s lender. Its primary purpose is to protect the tenant’s interest in the leased premises should the landlord’s mortgage lead to foreclosure.

1. Subordination

The subordination provision establishes that the tenant’s lease is subordinate to the lender’s mortgage. In simpler terms, if the landlord defaults on its loan, the lender takes precedence over the leased premises, and the tenant cannot prevent the lender from foreclosing and taking possession of the property.

2. Non-disturbance

The non-disturbance provision ensures that the lender will not disturb the tenant’s lease as long as the tenant complies with the lease terms. The tenant can continue to occupy the premises after a foreclosure sale, even if the lender sells the property to a new owner.

3. Attornment

The attornment provision requires the tenant to recognize the lender as the new landlord following a foreclosure sale. This means paying rent to the lender and abiding by the lease terms.

Why SNDAs are essential for tenants

SNDAs provide assurance and stability in the face of foreclosure. Without one, tenants risk eviction if the lender forecloses on the landlord’s mortgage, which could disrupt business operations and result in significant financial losses.

SNDAs can also help tenants negotiate more favorable lease terms. Landlords are more inclined to offer advantageous conditions to tenants willing to sign an SNDA, because SNDAs make the leased premises more appealing to lenders.

How to secure an SNDA

Not every tenant can secure an SNDA. Eligibility often depends on the amount of space being leased, the lease term, and the tenant’s financial commitment, especially in markets like New York City.

The most effective way to obtain an SNDA is to negotiate it as part of the lease agreement. If the landlord is initially unwilling, tenants may explore other negotiation avenues, such as seeking a lower rent or an extended lease term.

Obtaining an SNDA after lease signing is more challenging, because both the landlord and the lender must be convinced to sign.

Tenant tip

Start negotiating the SNDA early in the leasing process to maximize your leverage.

In conclusion

An SNDA is a vital document that shields tenants from the upheaval and financial repercussions of a foreclosure. Tenants should proactively negotiate an SNDA as part of the lease. If that is not possible during initial negotiations, securing one after signing may present additional hurdles, but its importance in safeguarding tenant rights cannot be overstated.
